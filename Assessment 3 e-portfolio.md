## Summary of the Artefact
This Australian Government report discusses the public consultation on automated decision-making (ADM) in government services. The consultation ran from November 2024 to January 2025 and collected feedback from the public, experts and government organisations. It highlighted the importance of transparency, fairness and accountability. The report also found that people wanted clear explanations, human review and opportunities to challenge automated decisions (Attorney-General's Department 2025, pp. 1–3).

## Justification on the Artefact
I chose this artefact because it helped me understand how ADM can affect people who depend on government services. Before reading it, I mainly thought automation was about saving time. Now I realise that fairness and human oversight are equally important. This connects with Kantian ethics because people deserve respect and fair treatment, even when computers make decisions.

## Summary of the Artefact
This Services Australia report explains how automation and artificial intelligence can improve government services. It discusses using these technologies to process claims faster, reduce repetitive tasks and help staff support people with complex needs. The strategy also highlights the importance of fairness, transparency, human oversight and protecting customer information when using automated systems (Services Australia 2025, pp. 4–5).

## Justification on the Artefact
I chose this artefact because it helped me understand the benefits and risks of automated decision-making in government. Before reading it, I mainly thought automation was about reducing workloads. Now I realise it can also improve people's access to services. This connects with Act Utilitarianism because technology should benefit society while minimising harm. As a future ICT professional, I believe human oversight is important.

## Summary of the Artefact
This Australian Human Rights Commission report discusses how automated decision-making (ADM) can affect people's rights. It uses the Robodebt failure as an example of the harm caused by poorly managed government technology. The report recommends human rights assessments, clear explanations of automated decisions and opportunities for people to challenge unfair outcomes (Australian Human Rights Commission 2025, pp. 3–4).

## Justification on the Artefact
I chose this artefact because it helped me understand that ADM can seriously affect people's lives when mistakes happen. Before reading it, I mainly focused on how automation improves efficiency. Now I realise that protecting human rights is equally important. This connects with Kantian ethics because everyone deserves fairness, dignity and respect. As a future ICT professional, I believe automated systems should always be accountable and allow human review.

## Summary of the Artefact
This ABC News video discusses concerns about AI regulation in Australia. Former Human Rights Commissioner Ed Santow explains why governments should not rely only on technology companies to protect people from AI-related harm. He raises concerns about whether companies will prioritise public safety when there is no financial benefit. The discussion highlights the importance of government responsibility and appropriate safeguards (Aiken 2025).

## Justification on the Artefact
I chose this video because it helped me understand why government oversight is important when AI influences decisions. Before watching it, I thought technology companies could manage most risks themselves. Now I realise that public protection also requires clear rules and accountability. This connects with Rule Utilitarianism because consistent regulations can help protect society. As a future ICT professional, I believe fairness and safety should come before efficiency.
